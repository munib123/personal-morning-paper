City council approves new cycling network

By A. Reporter, Saturday 17 October 2026

The city council voted on Friday evening to approve a 40-kilometre network of protected cycling lanes, the largest transport project the city has undertaken in more than a decade. The plan connects the university district with the central business area, the railway station and three large residential neighbourhoods on the eastern side of the river.

Supporters said the network would cut traffic on the busiest roads, reduce air pollution during the winter months and give students and shift workers a cheap and safe way to travel at any time of day. Several councillors pointed to surveys showing that many residents would cycle more often if they felt safer sharing the road with buses and lorries.

Opponents raised concerns about the loss of parking spaces along two commercial streets and asked for a longer consultation with shop owners before construction begins. The council agreed to publish a detailed map of every affected street and to hold weekly meetings with traders while the first section is being built next spring.

The project will be paid for by a mix of national transport grants and the city's own capital budget. Officials expect the first twelve kilometres to open within eighteen months, with the rest of the network following in stages over the next four years, depending on how quickly the contractors can complete the bridge crossings.

Transport experts said the decision placed the city among a growing group of mid-sized cities investing in separated bike infrastructure rather than painted lanes. They noted that similar networks elsewhere had doubled the number of daily cycle trips within five years, while also making streets calmer and more pleasant for people walking to local shops, schools and parks.
